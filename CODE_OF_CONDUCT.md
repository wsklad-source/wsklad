# Code of conduct

## Our pledge

We as members, contributors and maintainers pledge to make participation in this
project a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size,
visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity
and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status,
nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and
orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming,
diverse, inclusive and healthy community.

## Our standards

Behaviour that contributes to a positive environment:

- Being patient with people who are learning. A question asked badly is still a
  good question, and on a project with one maintainer, a question that reveals a
  documentation gap is a gift.
- Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback. When feedback is given,
  assume the reviewer read the code carefully, because on a one-person project
  they did.
- Taking responsibility for a mistake, saying so plainly, and fixing it.

Behaviour that is not acceptable:

- Sexualised language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind
- Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks
- Public or private harassment
- Publishing another person's private information, such as a physical or email
  address, or their real name, without their explicit permission
- Sustained disruption of discussions
- Dismissing or belittling a security report, an audit finding, or a user's bug
  report on the grounds that it is unlikely or inconvenient
- Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting

## Security reports are not conduct issues

Reporting a vulnerability is a contribution, and a welcome one. It is never a
reason for any kind of harassment, and it must never be made in a public issue —
see [`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md) for the channel. Reports are credited in the
advisory unless anonymity is requested.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in all project spaces — the repository, issues,
pull requests, discussions — and when an individual is officially representing
the project in public spaces.

## Enforcement

Report unacceptable behaviour to `support@wsklad.ru`. All complaints will be
reviewed and investigated, and will result in a response that is deemed necessary
and appropriate to the circumstances. The maintainer is obliged to keep the
identity and details of the reporter confidential.

The maintainer is not bound by this document in a dispute with a user of the
project, but is expected to follow it, and a maintainer who does not is removed
from the project.

## Attribution

version 2.1, available at
<https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct.html>.

The text is referenced rather than inlined so there is one authoritative version.
If the two ever disagree, the Contributor Covenant version 2.1 applies.

Two changes from the original, both narrowing scope: a single named maintainer is
the enforcement contact, and a "dismissing a security report" clause was added,
because in a security-sensitive project the most likely conduct problem is not
hostility between contributors but a reporter being told their finding is
inconvenient.
